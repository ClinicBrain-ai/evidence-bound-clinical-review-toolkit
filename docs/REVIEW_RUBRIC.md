# Review Rubric

This rubric supports consistent review of healthcare-adjacent AI outputs. It does not validate clinical correctness.

## Evidence Support

- Strong: Material claims are linked to supplied source details.
- Partial: Some claims are linked, but important statements need clearer support.
- Weak: Claims are mostly unsupported or rely on information not present in the source.

## Missing Information

- Low concern: Missing details are minor and do not affect review routing.
- Moderate concern: Missing details limit confidence in the output.
- High concern: Missing details are central to the output and require escalation or rejection.

## Unsupported Claims

- None identified: No unsupported material claims are found.
- Some identified: Unsupported claims are present but can be revised or qualified.
- Significant: Unsupported claims materially change the meaning or risk of the output.

## Scope Creep

- In scope: The output stays within summary, organization, or review-support language.
- Borderline: The output implies clinical interpretation beyond the source facts.
- Out of scope: The output suggests diagnosis, treatment, patient advice, or autonomous clinical judgment.

## Risk Level

- Low: Administrative or clearly non-clinical review-support content.
- Medium: Healthcare-adjacent content with uncertainty, missing details, or potential clinical interpretation.
- High: Content involving possible urgent symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, medication, patient-specific advice, or unsupported clinical claims.

## Review Requirement

- Required: Human review is needed before downstream use.
- Escalation required: Specialist or designated clinical reviewer input is needed.
- Not eligible for use: Output should not proceed without substantial revision or replacement.

## Reviewer Decision

- Approve with no changes: Output is acceptable for the intended non-clinical review context.
- Approve with revisions: Output may proceed only after specified edits.
- Reject: Output should not be used.
- Escalate: Output requires review by an appropriate human reviewer before any use.
